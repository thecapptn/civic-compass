How this works

Everything below is published so you can judge our information rather than trust it. Where we are weak, we say so on this page and in the product itself.

Where information comes from

Sources are ranked by who has administrative authority over the fact in question, not by reputation. Authority is specific: a state agency is the authority for statewide registration rules but not for a city drop-box location.

  1. The responsible election authority — your county, city, or state election office. This is the only source that can make a fact “official” here.
  2. Federal references — USA.gov, the U.S. Election Assistance Commission, and FVAP for national explanations and military and overseas voting. NASS TrustedInfo and CanIVote.org help locate the responsible office.
  3. Civic data partners — used only to widen coverage, always labelled “aggregated,” never presented as confirmed.
  4. Our own explanations — plain-language definitions only. We never state a jurisdiction-specific legal fact in our own voice.

How freshness works

Every record has a window in which its verification stays valid. The window shortens as an election approaches, and it is shortest for the things that change late and matter most: polling places, deadlines, and results. When a record passes its window it is automatically downgraded to “Not yet confirmed.” We would rather show you a gap than a stale answer wearing a confident label.

When sources disagree

If two election authorities publish different information for the same fact, we show both, label the record as conflicting, and send you to the election office. We do not silently choose. In a checklist we display the earlier of two dates, because acting early is the recoverable error, and we say that we are doing it.

Neutrality

Civic Compass does not endorse, rank, recommend, or fundraise, and accepts no advertising or paid placement. Candidates and measures within a contest render through one shared template, so they cannot be given different space, weight, or imagery. Ordering is computed at page-render time from a rule that is stated on the page; there is no stored order and no ranking field in the database to edit. Party labels appear as text and never as colour.

Candidates receive identical questions in identical order. An unanswered question is shown as “No response provided.” We do not describe that as a refusal, and we do not interpret it.

Privacy

We store: saved plans, if you choose to create one — a random link with an expiry date, holding only a jurisdiction, an election, and a voting method. Correction reports, and an email address only if you choose to give one, which is deleted when the report is resolved.

We do not store: the address or ZIP code you enter. It is used to work out your jurisdictions inside a single request and then discarded. It is never placed in a web address, never written to a log, and never sent to a third party.

We never record or infer: your party, your political views, which candidates you looked at, or anything derived from them. The database has no field for it.

We do not: run advertising, sell or share location data, use third-party analytics, or send notifications about candidates.

What we do not know

We do not know whether you are registered. We do not know which precinct you are assigned to unless an election office publishes it. We do not know about a late court order, an emergency polling-place change, or a candidate withdrawal until a source publishes it and we ingest it. For anything time-critical, the election office is faster and more reliable than we are.
